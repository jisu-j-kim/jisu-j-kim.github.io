About

I am an Assistant Professor of Marketing at Auburn University. My research examines how firms build and sustain customer relationships as the technologies mediating those relationships evolve, spanning loyalty programs, influencer and platform strategies, and emerging technologies such as generative AI. I work primarily with empirical modeling of large-scale field data, complemented by experimental and qualitative methods, to understand how these technological shifts reshape exchanges between buyers and sellers.

My research appears in leading journals, including the Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science (JAMS) and the Journal of Retailing, and has been recognized with the Journal of Retailing's Davidson Award, which I received twice (2019, 2021), selection as a finalist for the JAMS/Sheth Foundation Best Paper Award (2021), and the AMS Review/Sheth Foundation Doctoral Competition Award (2017). I serve on the Editorial Review Board of JAMS and as an ad hoc reviewer for several other leading marketing journals.

At Auburn, I developed and currently teach the Customer Relationship Management (CRM) course, and I have taught marketing at the undergraduate, graduate, and executive levels. In my classroom, I emphasize learning by doing: students work hands-on with the software tools they will encounter in practice, such as Salesforce, so that course concepts connect directly to how CRM is actually done.

Beyond academia, I have consulted for organizations including U.S. federal agencies, Fortune Global 500 companies, and major Korean firms. I hold a Ph.D. in Marketing from the University of Washington, an M.S. in Management Engineering from KAIST, and a B.S. in Information Systems from Carnegie Mellon University.

In my spare time, I like to try my hand at viral recipes — Dubai chewy cookies, baked feta pasta, whatever the algorithm serves up next.
